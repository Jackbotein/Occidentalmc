G-010The problem

Most staffing firms are paid to fill roles, not to find durable long-term fits.

A lot of data center recruiting gets run out of offshore call centers where recruiters have a daily quota of resumes to send. They're selling volume, and it works fine for them, because when a hire falls apart it's your schedule that slips and your liquidated damages clause that gets tested, not theirs.

01Submissions built to a quota

When someone's measured on how many resumes they send, a bad fit still counts toward the number. So they keep coming, and you keep reading them.

02Early departures

If the only reason someone took the job was the number, they'll leave when the next recruiter calls with a bigger one. You've already paid the fee, you've lost the ramp-up, and now you're hiring again in the middle of the build.

03Disqualifying issues found late

Badging, clearances, non-competes, and how much travel someone will actually accept tend to come up after the offer goes out, which is exactly when you have no time to start over.

T-100Sourcing thesis

Despite the immense labor demand, there is underutilized talent in America right now.

We identify them systematically using data. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed July 4, 2025, ended the residential 25D credit for anything installed from January 1, 2026, and gave wind and solar projects until July 4, 2026 to start construction if they wanted the 48E and 45Y credits. Renewable EPC firms had staffed up for a completely different schedule. Those crews are available now, and the work they were doing is a lot closer to a data hall than most hiring managers realize.

None of this is a talent problem, it's a policy cycle. The superintendent who ran a 200 MW solar build didn't get worse at his job in July. We go looking wherever a schedule just broke, which is usually months before those people turn up on a job board.

Human Capital Radar

We built a tool (Human Capital Radar) to identify talent that has been lost to cyclical sector slowdowns, like renewables, and then we reach out to them. It watches bankruptcy dockets on PACER and state WARN notices, flags the renewable EPC and installer filings, and sorts the affected workforce into the MEP phases our clients hire for.

Each one opens straight into that employer's staff list, and we start calling from there, usually within 48 hours of the filing.

We only publish company-level public records here. We don't list candidates by name, and we don't tell anyone that a particular person is looking.
